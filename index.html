What are phrasal verbs?

What it meansA short page for learners: what a phrasal verb is, why it is useful, how the little word helps, where it goes, and how to use this app.

Little words

What it meansThe verbs grouped by their little word, such as all the verbs with up.

Order on its screenin, out, up, down, on, off, back, away, over. Opposites sit next to each other. This order is judgement. Inside a little word, ★ verbs come first.

Little word meanings

What it meansNine little words, each split into the meanings it has in many phrasal verbs, such as UP means finished. A verb can be in two of these if it has two meanings; it then shows only the meaning that fits. Meanings that fit none of these are under Other at the end. The meanings, and which verb goes under which, are judgement.

Order on its screenup, down, on, off, in, out, back, away, over. Up and down come first because their meanings are the clearest. Inside a meaning, ★ verbs come first. The other little words come after these nine, with their verbs but no meanings yet.

Where it goes

What it meansWith many phrasal verbs, a short word like it, them, me or him must go between the verb and the little word: put it down, not put down it. A longer word can go in either place: put down your bag and put your bag down are both correct.

Where you see itThe meanings where this matters end with Word order: a ✓ next to the right way and a ✗ next to the wrong way. The short word matches the example sentence where it can.

Where you do not see itMeanings with no object, like break down or get up. Verbs where the object always goes at the end, like look after them. Verbs with three words, like put up with.

    What are phrasal verbs?

    A phrasal verb is a verb and a little word that go together: get up, turn off, look after. Together they have a new meaning.

    A new meaning

    You often cannot guess the meaning from the two words.

    lookuse your eyes. Look at the board.

    look upfind information. I looked up the word in the dictionary.

    look aftertake care of. She looks after her dad.

    Some phrasal verbs have three words: put up with (accept something bad), look forward to (be happy about something in the future).

    Why learn them?

    People use phrasal verbs all the time when they speak English. They are in films, songs, work and shops.

    There is often a longer word with the same meaning. The longer word is more formal. It is good for writing and forms. The phrasal verb is good for speaking.

    Speakingfind out · put off · come back · go up

    Writing and formsdiscover · postpone · return · increase

    The little word helps you

    The little word often has the same meaning in many phrasal verbs. UP can mean finished: eat up, use up, fill up. OFF can mean stop: turn off, switch off. If you know the little word, you can often guess a new verb.

    To see this, choose Little word meanings on the home screen.

    One verb, many meanings

    Some phrasal verbs have more than one meaning. Pick up can mean lift (Pick up your bag), collect (I pick up my son at 3) or learn (She picked up English quickly). Look at the sentence to find the meaning.

    Where does it go?

    With many phrasal verbs, a short word like it or them goes in the middle.

    ✓ Turn it off. ✗ Turn off it.

    ✓ Turn off the light. ✓ Turn the light off.

    In this app, Word order shows you where it goes.
